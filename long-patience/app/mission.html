Lighthugger Long Patience

Your navigation computer has failed. Before the next waypoint you must measure your own speed, then send a courier to the moving colony ship Nova Archimedes without slowing down.

First, explore the relativistic effects behind your instruments.

1 · Length contraction

The station has set out marker buoys 200 m apart. Choose a frame; the diagram compares positions measured simultaneously in that frame.

View both the station frame and ship frame to continue.

2 · Time dilation

Each buoy carries a clock. The station has synchronized them all. The ship carries one clock past them.

3 · Your speedometer

Cosmic-ray collisions can produce muons, but a muon at rest survives only about 2.2 microseconds. Here an artificial guidepost beam provides muons at rest in the guidepost frame; the slider sets their speed relative to your ship. Muons enter the 1200 m bore, decay, and produce electrons that flash blue Cherenkov light in the ice. Faster relative speeds mean longer lifetimes in the ship frame, so they get farther.

4 · Light as a speedometer

Choose a frame. The lamp is a hydrogen line source; the hull is a 3,200 K blackbody. Both spectra shift by the same Doppler factor, so a line and a continuum give the same speed.
